SECTION A — TITLE AND ONE-LINE DEFINITION

Finding Memory
AI Discovery Engine for Incomplete-Memory Visual Retrieval

Finding Memory turns public accounts of searching for remembered visual items into traceable evidence about the clues people use, the paths they take, and the problems that deserve further research.

Primary product context: Google Photos.
Project stage: Product discovery, before final problem selection, root-cause validation, solution ideation, feature design, MVP prototyping or PRD creation.
Document status: Draft for human approval. This document specifies a future research tool; it does not report completed research or authorize implementation.
Version: 1.1. Updated 6 October 2026 to require quantitative and qualitative dashboard visualisations.

SECTION B — OVERVIEW

People use visual libraries to keep memories and useful information. Later, they may know what they want to recover without remembering the words, date or location that would help them find it. Their starting point might be a person's relationship to them, a colour, a conversation, a life period, a text fragment or an approximate scene.

Finding Memory studies this behaviour through public accounts of actual retrieval attempts. It examines successful, partly successful, unsuccessful and abandoned searches, as well as attempts whose outcome is unreported. Difficulty is a research question, not a condition imposed on every record.

Google Photos is the primary context because it is the product chosen for this project. Comparisons with other photo libraries and visual-content platforms help distinguish problems shared across products from problems tied to a particular search experience, media type or storage arrangement. Inclusion does not imply a verified market-share ranking.

The engine serves researchers studying retrieval; it does not search a consumer's personal photo library. Its input is permitted public evidence and supporting reference material. Its output is inspectable cases, patterns, tentative explanations and questions for primary research.

The governing principle is: evidence first, interpretation second, solution later. Natural-language search, visual search, automated questioning, recognition-led browsing and chat interfaces are possible topics to investigate, not predetermined answers. Neither Google Photos search quality nor any preferred feature concept is assumed.

SECTION C — PROBLEM CONTEXT

The situation under study begins when someone knows or strongly believes that a specific visual item exists and wants to retrieve it, but their initial memory is incomplete, approximate, uncertain or difficult to express.

The target may be a photo, video, screenshot, receipt, ticket, document, ID image, downloaded image, meme, forwarded image, saved social post, reference image or another visually stored item. The desired result may be an exact item rather than any image of the same event or subject.

Illustrative scenarios, not research findings or participant quotations:

- I remember a photo with my cousin, but not when it was taken.
- I remember a phrase in a screenshot, but not the date I saved it.
- I can picture the image but cannot describe it clearly.
- I saw the photo recently in Memories and cannot find it again.
- I remember the event but not its place or year.
- I know the image exists somewhere, but not which account or application contains it.

Memory may also change during retrieval. Seeing a nearby image might prompt a new clue; an initially confident date might be revised. These possibilities must be studied without treating a plausible sequence as an observed one.

The existence of the target is usually a user belief in public evidence. The engine cannot independently establish that it was saved, indexed or accessible unless the source provides that information. Missing results must not automatically become evidence of a memory failure or a search-system defect.

SECTION D — GOAL

Build a research engine that gathers real, permitted public evidence; identifies relevant retrieval cases; preserves what users actually report; structures cues, uncertainty, actions, journeys and outcomes; and supports traceable comparison and analysis. The resulting shortlist of problem opportunities must help decide what to investigate through interviews, live retrieval tasks, usability studies or diary research.

Central research question:

“When users know a visual item exists but cannot precisely describe it at the start of retrieval, what fragments of memory are available to them, how do those cues evolve during the retrieval process, where does the existing retrieval experience stop supporting them, and which recurring breakdown represents the strongest opportunity to improve successful retrieval?”

Supporting questions include what distinguishes successful attempts, when uncertain clues mislead retrieval, what triggers reformulation, how users verify an exact match, and when another account, app, device or person enters the journey.

The engine must support this research chain:
Raw evidence → relevant cases → memory cues → behaviours → journeys → breakdowns and workarounds → outcomes → patterns → root-problem hypotheses → research opportunities.

At this stage, the project does not select a final consumer feature, prove a causal root problem, estimate population prevalence from public posts, or replace primary research. Creating this document does not start collection, implementation or deployment.

SECTION G — DATA SOURCE STRATEGY

Maintain three independently labelled corpora with separate record totals, retrieval filters and reporting denominators.

| Corpus | Contents | Permitted use | Counting rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| USER_EVIDENCE | Authentic accounts of retrieval attempts, including relevant context and outcomes. | Behavioural coding, journeys, patterns and corpus-level counts. | Count only qualifying, unique evidence; distinguish main cases from controls and context. |
| PRODUCT_REFERENCE | Official product help, capability documentation and release information. | Explain intended support, prerequisites and changes in product context. | Never count as user behaviour or towards the 2,000-record target. |
| COGNITIVE_REFERENCE | Academic literature and clearly labelled educational references. | Frame questions and interpret possible mechanisms cautiously. | Never count as a user case or proof that a mechanism occurred in one. |

In a help thread, the original user's account may be USER_EVIDENCE, while an official capability explanation is reference/context. Classification happens at the relevant content level rather than assigning every message the role of the page hosting it.

RAG may use multiple corpora when a question requires them, but must label the contribution of each. Corpus statistics must never blend the three. Reference-supported explanations and user-reported experiences may disagree; preserve both.

SECTION L — COGNITIVE_REFERENCE CORPUS

Collect literature covering retrieval cues, cue-dependent recall, encoding specificity, recognition versus recall, episodic memory, autobiographical memory, visual imagery, reconstructive memory, interference, source-memory errors, context-dependent retrieval, and memory accessibility versus availability.

Prioritise peer-reviewed primary studies and reviews, academic journals, academic books, PubMed/PMC and reputable university sources. Secondary educational material may support orientation but must be labelled and must not anchor major research claims where stronger evidence is needed.

For each reference, record the research question, study population, task, central result, limitations, citation/DOI when available and relevance to this project. Access through PubMed/PMC does not by itself establish quality or applicability.

Use cognitive concepts to ask better questions and consider alternative explanations. Do not diagnose a user's memory, equate search failure with a cognitive deficit, or claim that recognition always outperforms recall. A study of laboratory memory does not directly establish behaviour in a personal photo library.

The current document defines topics for later literature collection. It does not claim that a literature review has been completed or that any cognitive mechanism is validated for the target users.

SECTION M — INCLUSION CRITERIA

Accept a main-corpus case when the available account supports all of the following:

1. A user knows or believes a specific visual item exists or was encountered.
2. The user wants or wanted to retrieve that item.
3. The target is an eligible visual-media item.
4. Retrieval involves supported incomplete, approximate, uncertain, potentially incorrect or difficult-to-articulate memory; cue evolution; recognition as part of retrieval; browsing after a relevant search difficulty; or source/account uncertainty.

Prefer accounts containing at least one concrete cue, action, query, transition or outcome beyond generic praise or criticism. No complete journey is required. Ambiguous cases remain in a review queue and are excluded from accepted statistics until resolved.

Separate categories:

- MAIN_INCOMPLETE_MEMORY: qualifies under the rules above.
- PRECISE_MEMORY_SYSTEM_FAILURE: the user reports a precise cue, such as an exact keyword, and search still fails. This is a control for comparing memory-related difficulty with possible system or coverage problems; it does not prove a technical defect.
- CONTEXT_ONLY: relevant advice or background without an eligible personal retrieval account.
- EXCLUDED or NEEDS_REVIEW: outside scope or unresolved.

Controls and context must have separate denominators and do not satisfy the main 2,000-record target. Advice, feature requests and official replies may contextualise a case but must not masquerade as an attempted action.

SECTION N — EXCLUSION CRITERIA

Exclude backup-only issues, deleted-photo recovery, storage pricing, generic crashes, upload issues, sharing problems, editing problems, privacy complaints, sync-only issues, account login problems and generic sentiment without retrieval behaviour from the main corpus.

An otherwise excluded issue may be retained when it directly affects an eligible retrieval journey. For example, a searcher uncertain about which account contains an image may qualify; inability to sign in with no remembered-item retrieval attempt does not. Record the interaction rather than treating every account complaint as source uncertainty.

Also exclude general browsing, finding any attractive image, fictional examples presented as evidence, duplicated accounts, unverified imported text and unsupported feature speculation. Hypothetical wishes can be contextual material but are not evidence of actual attempts.

Give every excluded record one primary exclusion reason and optional secondary reasons. Retain collection accounting without retaining unnecessary content. Do not discard a record because it is short, positive, negative, contains emojis or describes success.

SECTION O — REQUIRED EVIDENCE VOLUME

Target: at least 2,000 unique, verified, relevant USER_EVIDENCE records in the main incomplete-memory corpus after deduplication, relevance filtering and source verification.

An evidence record is a source message or review, not an extracted sentence, cue, chunk or AI summary. Multiple eligible messages can document one case; do not count them as independent experiences. Context-only comments do not increase the target. Report record count, distinct retrieval-case count and conversation count separately.

Verification establishes that retained content came from the identified source and is relevant under the documented rules. It does not prove every reported event or independently confirm the user's outcome. Human-reviewed interpretation is a separate status.

If the target is not reached, report collected records, unique records, duplicates, exclusions by reason, unresolved records, verified qualifying records, control records, independent cases, inaccessible sources and the shortfall reason. Use an explicit collection waterfall and a corpus snapshot date.

Never synthesize reviews, expand one story into several invented cases or loosen scope to meet the target. Quality and provenance override the quota. A smaller corpus may support a useful demonstration, but the volume criterion must remain visibly unmet.

SECTION Q — MEMORY-CUE MODEL

Support cues concerning person, relationship, approximate time, exact date, life period, event, location, relative location, object, clothing, colour, background, action, emotion, weather, season, occasion, who took or sent the item, app/source, purpose for saving it, before/after context, visible text, event sequence, conversation context, device, media format, visual layout/composition, a similar/reference image, and other emergent clues.

For each cue preserve its type, source wording, normalised description, source span, owner/speaker, reported journey position, state, origin and evidence label. Do not turn an approximate year into a precise date or a relationship into an invented identity.

| Cue state | Meaning | Evidence requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CONFIDENT | The user presents the cue with expressed certainty. | Preserve that expression; confidence is not proof the cue is correct. |
| APPROXIMATE | The user gives a rough range or estimate. | Keep the range or qualifying language. |
| UNCERTAIN | The user expresses doubt. | Retain words indicating uncertainty. |
| POSSIBLY_WRONG | The user questions or revises a clue as potentially incorrect. | Link the doubt or correction to its source. |
| CONTRADICTORY | Accounts of the same cue conflict. | Preserve both versions and indicate whether the conflict is resolved. |
| FORGOTTEN | The user explicitly states they cannot remember the cue. | An unmentioned cue never qualifies. |
| NEWLY_RECALLED | A clue becomes available later in the reported attempt. | Support its later emergence and link the transition if reported. |

These states need not be mutually exclusive. A newly recalled clue may remain uncertain. Unqualified statements are recorded as stated without explicit certainty, rather than automatically marked CONFIDENT. Not mentioned, not applicable and unknown are distinct from FORGOTTEN.

The engine must not infer demographic, medical or other sensitive attributes from cues. A reference to a cousin or friend is task context, not a basis for identifying that person.

SECTION R — CUE EVOLUTION

Cue evolution is a core research dimension. The engine must examine whether a clue is added, refined, contradicted, discarded or corrected during retrieval and whether the source identifies what triggered that change.

Required concepts:

- initially_accessible_cues: clues reported as available at the start, not all clues mentioned anywhere in the thread.
- new_cue_trigger: a reported result, action or event preceding a newly recalled or revised clue.
- newly_recalled_cues: supported later additions.
- cue_evolution_sequence: the ordered cue changes with supporting message/span references.
- did_recognition_trigger_recall: yes, no or unknown, with evidence and distinction between an explicit statement and an interpretation.
- external_cue_trigger: a trigger outside the current search results, when reported.

Potential triggers include another search result, a nearby photo, a surfaced Memory, another device, another application, another person, a notification, a physical event or another related image. These are observation categories, not assumed effects.

Seeing a result before mentioning a clue does not establish that the result caused recall. When the relationship is only plausible, label it AI_INTERPRETATION or RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS. An account containing one final query cannot establish the user's initial memory.

Use explicit changes to challenge early coding. Later correction must not silently replace the original cue; both belong to the same evolving case.

SECTION S — RETRIEVAL JOURNEY MODEL

A journey represents one reported attempt to recover a target item. A source record, conversation and retrieval journey are different units: one thread may contain several attempts, and several messages may describe one attempt.

An illustrative sequence is need → vague memory → first search → poor results → newly accessible clue → reformulation → browsing/filtering → account/app switch → workaround → outcome. It is a starting model, not a required path.

For every supported step capture the action, query or clue where available, actor, product/account context at a non-identifying level, step order, reported system response, source support and certainty of ordering. Keep an attempted workaround separate from advice offered by someone else.

Allow loops, skipped stages, resumed attempts and partial journeys. A post that only describes scrolling should not acquire an invented first search. Collection timestamps must not substitute for the time of the attempt.

Outcome labels:

- FOUND: user reports finding the intended item; mark self-reported confirmation.
- PARTIAL: a related item or some needed information is found, while the exact target remains unresolved.
- FAILED: user explicitly reports an unsuccessful attempt without confirming abandonment.
- ABANDONED: user reports stopping or deferring the attempt.
- NOT_REPORTED: the account does not give an outcome.
- UNCLEAR: outcome language is present but cannot be reliably interpreted.

No reply is NOT_REPORTED, not failure. Finding the correct event is not necessarily finding the exact photo. Later success can update the case through a versioned annotation while preserving earlier failed stages.

SECTION T — RETRIEVAL BREAKDOWN TAXONOMY

Starter codes include memory limitation, query-articulation difficulty, visual-to-verbal representation gap, query-understanding failure, metadata mismatch, indexing/tagging failure, OCR failure, person-recognition gap, ranking failure, incomplete result set, similar-results overload, date uncertainty, location uncertainty, source/account uncertainty, cross-platform fragmentation, browsing/navigation overload, combined problems and unknown.

These are provisional starter codes. Researchers must be able to add emergent codes, revise definitions and retain unclassified cases. The engine must not force every record into a predetermined category or treat multiple labels as mutually exclusive.

Keep three levels distinct:

1. Observed or reported symptom: the user did not see the desired item after a particular action.
2. Breakdown classification: the supported difficulty, such as uncertainty about the date or navigating many similar results.
3. Root-cause hypothesis: a possible explanation requiring additional validation.

Technical codes such as OCR, indexing or ranking failure require appropriate evidence. If a user says a remembered word returned no result, the symptom is supported; OCR failure remains a possible explanation alongside wrong memory, absent content, scope limitations or other causes.

Each code needs a definition, inclusion boundary, exclusion boundary and source-backed examples once collected. Codebook changes must be versioned so historical comparisons remain interpretable.

Minimise unnecessary personal identifiers before durable retention; a required withdrawal or deletion overrides ordinary immutability and must invalidate affected derivatives.

SECTION V — AI ANALYSIS REQUIREMENTS

Store analysis as separately versioned annotations linked to evidence and retrieval cases. Do not overwrite source content.

Required conceptual information includes:

- Identity and audit: analysis_id, evidence_id, related case/message IDs, model_name, analysis_prompt_version, analysed_at and human-review status.
- Scope: retrieval_relevance, relevance_score where evaluated, target_media_type and what_user_wanted_to_find.
- Memory: remembered_cues, forgotten_cues, uncertain_cues, possibly_wrong_cues, cue_states and cue_origin.
- Actions: initial_query, search_strategy, subsequent_actions, external_cue_trigger, newly_recalled_cues, recognition_triggered_recall and workaround.
- Journey and interpretation: retrieval_outcome, failure_point, problem_code and root_cause_hypothesis.
- Reported impact: effort_signal and severity_signal.
- Support and uncertainty: evidence_type, confidence, source spans, extraction rationale and alternative interpretations.

For forgotten_cues, require an explicit inability-to-remember statement. For initial_query, require a reported actual query; otherwise record not reported. A paraphrase of the search goal must not be displayed as verbatim input.

Effort and severity require reported signals. Exact minutes, attempt counts or importance levels are entered only when supported; adjectives such as frustrating remain qualitative signals. Negative sentiment and low ratings do not automatically indicate severe retrieval harm.

Separate source verification, relevance, extraction certainty and pattern-level confidence. A model's self-rated number is not a calibrated probability. Numeric confidence should be introduced only with evaluation; otherwise show understandable labels and their basis. Unknowns remain unknown and are visible in analysis.

| STRONGLY_IMPLIED_BEHAVIOUR | Context strongly supports behaviour that is not directly stated. | Mark it as implied, explain the inference and allow review. |
| AI_INTERPRETATION | The model proposes a meaning, grouping or explanation. | Display separately from what the source says; do not treat it as a quote or fact. |
| RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS | A broader explanation or relationship needs testing. | Show supporting and challenging evidence and a validation question. |

Apply labels to claims, not only whole records. A direct quotation can sit beside an inferred failure point; the quotation must not lend false certainty to that inference.

Translations and shortened excerpts need separate labels. A summary written by the model is not the user's wording. Preserve original language and relevant negation/context when quoting.

Default quantitative claims about reported actions/outcomes should use DIRECT_STATEMENT support. If an analysis includes strongly implied behaviour, disclose that choice and provide a direct-only comparison where useful. Hypotheses and unreviewed AI explanations must never become counts of confirmed events.

A commenter describing their own experience may contribute a separate case, but does not determine the original poster's outcome. A developer or moderator reply is not a consumer retrieval account.

SECTION AA — CLEANING REQUIREMENTS

Create a cleaned analysis derivative while preserving the meaning and provenance of the source capture under the source's retention conditions.

Preserve emojis, negation, uncertainty markers, query wording, slang and short retrieval accounts when they carry meaning. SECTION AB — DEDUPLICATION

Detect repeated source records, duplicate exports, equivalent canonical URLs with identical message identity, duplicated comments, syndicated reviews and confidently repeated cross-posted accounts.

Use source IDs and exact content checks first. Near-duplicate text is a candidate for review, not proof of duplication. Similar complaints from different people are independent evidence. Edited messages may be versions of one source record rather than new cases.

Maintain duplicate_group_id, the canonical retained record, aliases to original source locators, the deduplication reason and review status. Preserve useful extra context without counting the same account repeatedly.

Do not merge every review sharing an app-listing URL or every reply sharing a thread URL. Record identity requires the review/message locator and other source information.

Statistics operate on canonical unique evidence. Counts of independent cases must additionally account for messages describing the same attempt and confident repeated accounts across threads. Chunking for RAG, translations, embeddings and reanalysis never create additional evidence.

Uncertain cross-post duplication must remain explicit, with a sensitivity note if it materially affects a finding. Do not identify or link people across platforms to solve deduplication.

SECTION AD — QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS

Default rules:

- Cue or behaviour frequency: count each eligible case once per category, not each repeated mention.
- Co-occurrence: specify that the cues appear within the same case; do not infer that they appeared together in the initial query.

SECTION AE — QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS

SECTION AR — SCHEDULER

Future refreshes may use GitHub Actions or another equivalent scheduler. This is an application capability specification, not a request to create a scheduled task now.

A run should collect from enabled sources, create a batch, normalise, deduplicate, classify, analyse, embed new or changed eligible content and refresh derived statistics/indexes.

Configure frequency, source windows, rate limits, budget, retry policy and stop conditions. Avoid continuous scraping. Incremental checkpoints and idempotent processing should prevent duplicate records and duplicate analysis after retries.

Do not overlap runs that would corrupt state. Surface partial-source failures and preserve previously valid data without pretending the refresh succeeded. Keep last attempted run, last successful run and latest usable snapshot separate.

Review source-permission and deletion requirements before refreshes. Retained data and derivatives must stay within applicable source terms. Researcher control must include pausing collection without losing existing permitted analysis.

Scheduled collection must not trigger document publishing or sharing by default. Google Docs delivery is a separate, explicitly scoped action.

SECTION AS — PRIVACY

Use permitted public evidence only. Do not access a personal photo library, private account, private message, private group or research participant's device as part of this engine.

Minimise retained personal identifiers. Do not unnecessarily store usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, personal account identifiers or device IDs. Source URLs may themselves expose identifiers; account for this in public display and exports.

When speaker continuity is necessary, use an opaque, source-scoped pseudonym or appropriately protected keyed identifier. Do not use simple unsalted username hashes as a promise of anonymity, link identities across platforms or infer sensitive attributes.

Keep task context that is necessary for interpretation without reproducing unrelated personal details. Avoid collecting attached personal images unless separately necessary and permitted; text evidence does not require a copy of the user's photo.

Separate researcher-only raw/context access from the public demonstration. Apply source-specific retention, redaction, deletion and model-processing conditions. Use providers/settings that do not repurpose the research corpus for model training without an applicable basis.

Record legitimate removals and refresh affected statistics, embeddings and reports. Ordinary source immutability protects analytical integrity; it must not prevent required privacy or source-policy deletion.

SECTION AT — SOURCE COMPLIANCE

Prohibit scraping behind login, CAPTCHA bypass, anti-bot circumvention, access-limit evasion, private-group/private-message collection, leaked datasets and claims of API permissions that do not exist.

Before enabling a source, document the permitted access route, approval status, terms/reference URL, applicable rate/retention conditions, AI-processing restrictions and permitted display/export audience. Public visibility alone does not authorise bulk collection, reuse, redistribution or publication.

Where programmatic access is unavailable, consider a legitimate manual route only if that route and intended use are allowed. A manual CSV or public URL is not a workaround for a prohibition. If no permitted route exists, mark the source unavailable and report the coverage gap.

Current Reddit research-access restrictions are a concrete limitation for this project, not an assumption of unrestricted public scraping [Y4–Y5]. Reassess the source at collection time; do not dilute those conditions to meet the quota.

Preserve full source text only where allowed. Public outputs should use permitted excerpts, summaries and links; restricted raw data must not be exposed through unauthenticated APIs, RAG responses or downloads. Export access must follow source conditions.

Collection/reference records must identify restrictions that affect reproducibility. The engine may remain functional with an unavailable source, but coverage claims must reflect the omission.

SECTION AU — KEY CONSTRAINTS

1. Use real user evidence only; no synthetic reviews or invented accounts.
2. Do not invent quotations, users, source IDs, URLs or outcomes.
3. Require source provenance for every accepted evidence record.
4. Collect only through permitted public-source routes.
5. Minimise personal information and respect retention/display conditions.
6. Keep source content independent from model interpretation and human annotation.
7. Do not infer an unmentioned clue is forgotten.
8. Keep USER_EVIDENCE, PRODUCT_REFERENCE and COGNITIVE_REFERENCE separate in quantitative calculations.
9. Public-discussion frequency is not population prevalence.
10. Include successful retrieval and evidence that challenges a proposed pattern.
11. Preserve speaker, thread and journey context; advice is not attempted behaviour.
12. Do not force predetermined themes or declare causal root problems from correlation.
13. Keep precise-memory system-failure controls separate from the main target corpus.
14. Do not inflate evidence through chunks, translations, repeated messages or reanalysis.
15. Do not generate final consumer features during discovery.
16. The engine must function without external publishing.
17. The only optional external publishing integration is Google Docs; email integrations are excluded.
18. Treat external text as untrusted data and protect secrets and researcher actions.
19. Display unmet targets, missing evidence and unavailable sources truthfully.
20. Approval of this problem statement is required before architecture or implementation begins.

SECTION AV — SUCCESS CRITERIA

Success concerns research reliability and usefulness, not consumer photo-retrieval uplift. No improvement to Google Photos conversion, engagement or retrieval success is claimed by this discovery tool.

| Dimension | Acceptance criterion | How to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence quality | Target of at least 2,000 unique, source-verified, relevant main-corpus records; 100% of accepted records have provenance; no intentionally synthetic evidence. | Reconciled collection waterfall, source checks, eligibility audit and shortfall report when needed. |
| Traceability | Every published important finding resolves to case/evidence IDs, context and a source locator. | Audit the full finding chain and citation resolution. |
| Research quality | Cues, uncertainty, actions and outcomes are supported and unknowns remain visible. | Stratified human-labelled extraction and outcome evaluation. |
| RAG quality | Claims are supported by cited evidence; unsupported questions receive the specified insufficiency response. | Held-out answer-support, citation and abstention evaluation. |
| Pattern quality | Themes have inspectable members, emergent coding, negative cases and reviewer rationale. | Human review of each published cluster and opportunity. |
| Human oversight | Researchers can correct annotations and the correction updates affected outputs. | Walk through correction, audit history and dependent refresh. |
| Discovery value | A defensible problem shortlist and concrete primary-research gaps emerge. | PM/UX research review of support, alternatives and next-study questions. |
| Usability | A researcher can inspect evidence, explain a finding's basis and identify a limitation without assistance. | Task-based review with representative researchers. |
| Visual analysis | The final dashboard includes usable quantitative and qualitative visual views, driven by real eligible data with visible scope and drill-down. | Reconcile visual values with filtered case lists and exported aggregates; review qualitative maps against source-supported annotations. |

Proposed evaluation targets for the later Evals.md, not measured results:

- Create at least 150 real, permitted, stratified gold-standard examples, including relevant cases, controls, exclusions, ambiguous cases and successes. Independently double-code at least 30 examples and adjudicate disagreements.
- Evaluate on held-out examples not used to tune prompts. Initial targets: relevance precision at least 90%, relevance recall at least 80%, and at least 90% correct support/status assignment for extracted cues, actions and outcomes. Publish denominators and slice-level errors.
- Test at least 50 held-out research questions covering supported qualitative questions, structured counts, contradictions and insufficient-evidence cases. Target at least 95% supported factual answer claims and at least 90% correct insufficiency handling; report errors rather than hiding them.
- Require 100% resolvable citations and no fabricated evidence, quotes or URLs. Any known unsupported claim in a published finding must be corrected regardless of an aggregate target.
- Review the shortlisted opportunities and their cited support before publication.
- Verify every published chart's category definitions, numerator, denominator, aggregation and missing-data treatment against its underlying eligible records. Filtering, counts/share controls and exports must agree with the same corpus snapshot.
- Check that qualitative theme memberships, excerpts, journey steps and cue changes resolve to supporting evidence, with hypotheses and negative cases clearly labelled.
- Confirm readable labels, accessible alternatives, keyboard interaction and truthful empty/insufficient-data states for the visual-analysis views.

These are project acceptance targets, not scientific benchmarks or guarantees. Refine evaluation design after source and corpus feasibility are established; record any change transparently. Meeting a classifier target does not establish population validity. Volume shortfall means that criterion remains unmet even if the other capabilities work.

SECTION AX — DEFINITION OF DONE

A researcher should eventually be able to:

1. Open the application and understand its discovery purpose.
2. Read the central research question and scope.
3. Inspect the corpus and distinguish records from cases.
4. Understand source coverage, inaccessible sources and collection limits.
5. Browse genuine, eligible evidence.
6. Filter evidence without losing the analysis scope.
7. Open an identifiable original source where it remains available.
8. Inspect supported remembered and uncertain cues.
9. Explore complete or partial retrieval journeys.
10. Explore recurring patterns and their member cases.
11. Compare products with visible coverage and feature context.
12. Compare media types with correct denominators.
13. Ask bounded research questions.
14. Receive evidence-backed answers or an insufficiency statement.
15. Inspect citations and their supporting source spans.
16. Inspect contradictory evidence, successes and negative cases.
17. Identify gaps requiring primary research.
18. Compare problem opportunities with uncertainty preserved.
19. Export evidence/report material within permitted access and reuse conditions.
20. Optionally publish a selected report to an authorised Google Docs destination.

Additionally, the researcher must be able to correct an interpretation and see the change reflected in dependent views. Documented evaluations must be available, and unmet criteria must be visible. A live link, high raw-record count or polished interface alone does not establish completion.

Visual-analysis completion additionally requires a researcher to inspect both quantitative and qualitative views, understand their scope and unknowns, filter them consistently, open the supporting cases and export selected visuals with context. The final dashboard must contain the implemented visual-analysis capabilities, with truthful insufficient-data states where evidence does not support a particular analysis.

At no point should a researcher have to trust an unexplained AI conclusion. Consumer retrieval improvement and final feature validation belong to later phases.

SECTION AY — WHAT THE PRODUCT IS NOT

Finding Memory is not an Ask Photos clone, a Google Photos feature prototype, a sentiment dashboard, a review summariser, a generic chatbot, a feature-request summariser, a solution generator, a synthetic user-research generator or a generic competitor-analysis dashboard.

The engine studies reported retrieval behaviour and structures evidence for discovery. It does not connect to private photo libraries, retrieve a consumer's actual photo, diagnose memory, prove a causal explanation from public discussion or validate a preferred feature concept.

Comparators and cognitive references support research interpretation. They do not turn the application into a comprehensive feature audit or substitute for direct user observation.

Current-step boundary: complete this docs/ProblemStatement_Finding_Memory.txt and stop. Architecture, source implementation, formal schemas, APIs, agents, frontend screens, scheduled jobs, external writes and deployment require the later approved workflow. No final user-facing Google Photos solution is proposed here.

SECTION AZ — FINAL SUMMARY

Finding Memory is a discovery engine for understanding how people recover visual items when they start with incomplete or uncertain memory. It brings together permitted public accounts, product documentation and cognitive research while preserving their different evidential roles. Researchers can inspect clues, actions, evolving journeys, outcomes and recurring breakdowns, then trace every important finding back to its source. The result is a defensible set of problem hypotheses and research gaps that guides primary research before a solution is designed.
